# PETRÓLEO (Karto, 1976) — Reconstructed Rules

**Source:** the original Karto rules booklet, *"PETRÓLEO" — REGRAS*, 9pp A5,
Fábrica de Jogos Karto, Rua Delfim Ferreira 698, Porto. Scanned copy supplied by the
project owner.

**Status:** rules **complete and verified** from the primary source. The only outstanding
gaps are printed on the *board* and *cards* rather than in the booklet — see §12.

This is a structured restatement for implementation, not a facsimile. Original Portuguese
terms are retained as the canonical names for board spaces and components.

---

## 1. Overview

2–6 players, ~50 minutes. Each player runs an international oil company prospecting in
**Kartolândia**, an imaginary country whose sole wealth is petroleum. The state divided
part of its territory, on land and at sea, into squares (*zonas de exploração*) and
licenses foreign companies to exploit them for a fee. It also levies taxes and grants
concessions. All business passes through the **Banco da Kartolândia**.

The booklet is explicit that this is **not primarily a game of luck** — it is a game that
tests business intelligence.

Currency: **Milhões de Kartos** (M). All values below are in millions.

---

## 2. Components

Physical appearance, from photographs of a real copy: towers are black moulded
derricks; oil reservoirs are grey cylindrical tanks stamped with their capacity
("6 M.T."); gas reservoirs are white domes; the tank truck is yellow and the
tanker blue; the game marker is a blue round pawn; licences are white tiles
carrying the company wordmark; cheques are printed "BANCO DA KARTOLÂNDIA" in
denominations of 1, 5, 10, 20 and 50 M.K.

## 3. Setup

1. Players nominate a **banker** among themselves. The banker holds all bank property and
   **gains no benefit** from the role. The banker pays, receives and distributes towers,
   reservoirs, licences, nationalisations, tankers and trucks, and polices play.
2. The banker shuffles the 60 cards and **removes cards down to the player count** using
   the table in §4. All 60 are used only at 6 players.
3. The banker deals **4 cards to each player**; the rest form a face-down deck on the
   board square marked *«coloque aqui as cartas com as costas voltadas para cima»*.
4. The banker gives each player **200 M Kartos** in cheques as starting capital.
5. Each player chooses an oil company: **SACOR, SONAP, ESSO, MOBIL, SHELL, BP**.
   Company licence sheets are *not* dealt out — licences are bought as needed.
6. The blue marker starts on **Passagem de Ano**.

Each card carries **two** indications: the **number of squares the blue marker advances**,
and **what may be bought or negotiated**.

### Card anatomy, from a photographed original

A "COMPRE UM CAMIÃO CISTERNA" card shows the *Jogos Karto* mark at the head, a
tab down the left edge carrying the type name and a circled number, the title
across the middle over a faint illustration, and **"AVANCE ⑦ CASAS"** down the
right edge. The tab number and the move number are the same. Movement is always
forward — the booklet's "sempre na direcção da seta" leaves no room for a
move-backwards card.

**There are no event cards.** The table above is the complete deck: seven
purchase types and nothing else. Nationalisation, fires, storms, taxes and oil
price swings are all *board spaces* (§9), not cards. Likewise, cards do not name
a company or a price — the booklet is explicit that company symbols are not
dealt out ("os símbolos das companhias não são distribuídos"), and every price
is fixed in §8 rather than varying card by card.

## 5. Turn sequence

The player to the **left of the banker** goes first: they place the blue marker on
*Passagem de Ano*, then draw, play and advance (steps 2–4 below). They do not resolve a
space, since the marker has only just been placed.

Every subsequent turn, in clockwise order:

1. **Resolve the space** the blue marker currently occupies (it was moved there by the
   *previous* player's card).
2. **Draw one card** from the deck and add it to hand.
3. **Play one card** face down beside the deck, carrying out what it says *if able and
   willing*.
4. **Advance the blue marker** by the number of squares the played card indicates. Turn
   ends.

The marker always travels around the squares surrounding the map, always in the direction
of the arrow.

**Turn order never changes.** Consequence of step 1: the space a player resolves is
determined by the previous player's card, not their own — the mover chooses where the
*next* player lands.

---

## 6. Negotiating cards

A card you cannot or do not wish to use may be sold to another company.

- If more than one rival is interested, **hold an auction (*leilão*) and sell to the
  highest bidder**.
- The buyer pays you the agreed price **and** must still pay the bank the listed cost of
  the item itself.
- The buyer may then play that card **on their own turn** — turn order is unaffected.
- **Any** card type may be negotiated if it cannot be used.
- If you cannot use a card and no rival will negotiate for it, **you lose the right to it**.

---

## 7. Development chain

To bring a square into production, strictly in this order:

1. Buy an **exploration licence** for a square (land or sea).
2. Buy a **prospecting tower** and place it on the licence.
3. Play a reservoir card to **return the tower to the bank** and replace it with an oil or
   gas deposit on the licence.
4. Only from step 3 does the square earn at *Passagem de Ano*.

A licence counts as **"in exploration"** once it bears a tower *or* a deposit — this
matters for confiscation (§9, space 2).

Tankers and trucks need **no licence purchase**: the licence comes with the vehicle purely
to identify the owner, and nothing is paid to the bank for it.

## 8. Prices and annual profits

| Asset | Cost | Annual profit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploration licence — land | 7 | — |
| Exploration licence — sea | 5 | — |
| Prospecting tower — land | 5 | — |
| Prospecting tower — sea | 12 | — |
| Oil reservoir 6 M.T. | 10 | 20 |
| Oil reservoir 4 M.T. | 7 | 14 |
| Oil reservoir 2 M.T. | 5 | 10 |
| Gas reservoir | 6 | 12 |
| Tank truck (*camião cisterna*) | 5 | 5 |
| Oil tanker (*petroleiro*) | 300 | 100 |

Note the inversion: sea licences are *cheaper* than land (5 vs 7) but sea towers cost more
than double (12 vs 5).

Annual profits are paid by the bank to **all** companies whenever the blue marker passes
or stops on *Passagem de Ano*. Towers and licences never earn.

**House rule sanctioned by the booklet:** at **4 players or fewer**, a second *Passagem de
Ano* may be activated on the opposite square bearing the same design, doubling payout
frequency.

### Tankers and partnership (*sociedade*)

A tanker card lets you buy a tanker if your capital allows. If you cannot afford one alone
you may form a partnership:

- **Alone:** place one of your licences inside the **porto** and the tanker on top of it.
- **With another company:** place two licences in the port, yours on top of theirs, then
  the tanker on top of both. **Your licence commands the operations.**
- **With the bank:** place your licence in the port, the tanker on it, and a **green
  marker** beside it.
- If more than one rival is interested, **you choose** the partner. If none is, deal with
  the bank.
- Partnership is always **50/50** in profits *and* losses.
- On your turn you may dissolve it, offering to buy your partner's share or sell your own.
  If the partner is uninterested you may sell your share to the bank: withdraw your licence
  and place a green marker. Your ex-partner thereafter partners with the bank.

### Trucks

Place a company licence in the **zona industrial** — outside the prospecting squares — and
a truck on top of it. Pay the bank 5.

## 10. Special opening rule

The **first** time the marker lands on space **3** or **5** (buy a licence at sea or on
land), a player may **double up** and buy two licences of that type. Once per player,
only at the start of the game, only the first time they land on such a space. Forget, and
the opportunity is lost.

---

## 11. End of game and scoring

- When the deck is exhausted, play continues until every player's hand is empty — that is,
  **every player takes exactly 10 turns**.
- *(Arithmetic check: 10 cards per player, starting hand 4, draw 1 and play 1 per turn.
  The deck of 6N cards empties after 6 turns each, then 4 more turns each are played from
  hand. 10 turns each exactly.)*
- **Bankruptcy:** a player who runs out of money abandons the game; the banker removes all
  that company's assets from the board.
- **Bank shortfall:** if the bank runs out of money it may borrow from one or more players,
  **paying 10% interest immediately**.
- **Winner:** greatest total capital, summing **towers + oil and gas reservoirs + tankers +
  trucks + cheques**.
- Assets count at their **fixed purchase price** as listed in §8.
- **Licences have no value at scoring.**
- A still-nationalised company counts only **half** its assets.

## 12. Remaining gaps — on the board and cards, not in the booklet

1. **Track length and layout.** The booklet numbers 20 *space types* but does not say how
   many physical squares surround the map, nor in what order the types appear, nor which
   square is opposite *Passagem de Ano* (needed for the ≤4-player second-payout rule).
2. **Per-card move values.** Each of the 60 cards is printed with the number of squares to
   advance. The booklet does not tabulate them.
3. **Map geography.** Number and arrangement of land vs sea prospecting squares, and the
   position of the **porto** and the **zona industrial**.

One value is now confirmed: a *camião cisterna* card carries **7**.

A legible photograph or scan of the board closes 1 and 3; photographs of the cards close 2.
